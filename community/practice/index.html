5 · Reading Comprehension

25 Q · 38 min on actual test

The four forces acting on an aircraft in steady, level flight are thrust, drag, lift, and weight. Thrust is the forward force produced by the engine; drag is the rearward resistance from the air. Lift is generated as air flows over and under the wings at different speeds, creating a pressure differential. Weight, the pull of gravity, acts downward through the center of mass. For steady, level flight, thrust must equal drag and lift must equal weight. When these forces are balanced, the aircraft maintains constant altitude and airspeed. Pilots control the balance by adjusting engine power and control surfaces.

  • EThrust must always be greater than lift
