All 12 Subtests

Timing, Tips, and Key Patterns

Master the format before you master the content. These are the highest-leverage tips per subtest.

1. Verbal Analogies
25 Q / 8 min
Verbal, Academic Aptitude
  • Build a bridge sentence: "A [verb] B, so C [verb] ___"
  • Relationship types: part-to-whole, cause-effect, synonym, antonym, function, category
  • Eliminate options that flip the relationship direction
  • 19 seconds per question — don't overthink
Common patterns: tool:function, item:category, word:antonym, cause:effect
2. Arithmetic Reasoning
25 Q / 29 min
Quantitative, Academic Aptitude
  • Word problems — underline the question, not just the numbers
  • Sketch the scenario (distance problems, mixture problems)
  • Watch unit conversions: miles vs. km, hours vs. minutes
  • Use d = rt, work = rate × time as default setups
d = r·t | work = r·t | % change = (new-old)/old · 100
3. Word Knowledge
25 Q / 5 min
Verbal, Academic Aptitude
  • 12 seconds per question — fastest subtest on the test
  • Use Latin/Greek roots to decode unknown words
  • Look for the "closest meaning" not the exact dictionary definition
  • Context clues help even without a passage
bene=good | mal=bad | pre=before | post=after | sub=under | super=above
4. Math Knowledge
25 Q / 22 min
Quantitative, Academic Aptitude, Pilot, CSO
  • Highest ROI subtest for Pilot composite — prioritize this
  • Know exponent rules, FOIL, factoring, quadratic formula cold
  • Geometry: area, perimeter, volume, Pythagorean theorem
  • Basic trig: SOH-CAH-TOA, unit circle values at 0/30/45/60/90
x = (-b ± √(b²-4ac)) / 2a | a²+b²=c² | sin/cos/tan = Opp/Hyp, Adj/Hyp, Opp/Adj
5. Reading Comprehension
25 Q / 38 min
Verbal, Academic Aptitude
  • Read the questions first — target your passage scanning
  • Correct answer is always directly supported by passage text
  • Beware "true in the real world but not stated here" traps
  • Main idea questions: first and last sentence of each paragraph
Strategy order: Questions → Skim passage → Find evidence → Eliminate wrong answers
6. Situational Judgment
50 Q / 35 min
Officer Leadership Composite
  • Think: what would a professional, ethical AF officer do?
  • Chain of command matters — report up before acting independently
  • Mission first, but never at the expense of safety or ethics
  • Avoid extremes: neither passive (do nothing) nor rogue (bypass chain)
Best answer: address issue professionally, within authority, through proper channels
7. Self-Description Inventory
220 Q / 40 min
Officer Leadership Composite
  • Personality assessment — there are no trick answers
  • Answer honestly and consistently — inconsistency is flagged algorithmically
  • Genuine leadership tendencies are what's measured
  • Don't overthink; your first instinct is usually right
Do not try to "game" this subtest — authentic responses score best
8. Physical Science
20 Q / 10 min
Academic Aptitude, CSO
  • Newton's 3 Laws: inertia, F=ma, equal and opposite reactions
  • Ohm's Law: V = IR | Power: P = IV = I²R
  • Know states of matter, atomic structure basics, periodic table trends
  • Speed of light: 3×10⁴ m/s | Speed of sound: ~343 m/s at sea level
F=ma | V=IR | E=mc² | KE=½mv² | PE=mgh | p=mv
9. Table Reading
40 Q / 7 min
Pilot, CSO
  • 10 seconds per question — pure speed drill
  • Find row (X value) and column (Y value), trace to intersection
  • Practice with grid paper under 10-second time pressure
  • Don't double-check unless you have time — first reading is usually right
Drill: find X on left axis → find Y on top axis → trace to intersection
10. Instrument Comprehension
25 Q / 5 min
Pilot, CSO
  • Read artificial horizon AND compass simultaneously
  • Artificial horizon: bar shows horizon; bank left = right wing high
  • Compass: 0/360=North, 90=East, 180=South, 270=West
  • Practice "level left bank right" mental model until automatic
N=0°/360° | E=90° | S=180° | W=270° | NE=45° | NW=315°
11. Block Counting
30 Q / 4.5 min
Pilot, CSO
  • Count how many blocks are touching the numbered block (shared faces)
  • Diagonal touching does NOT count — faces only
  • Work top-to-bottom, front-to-back systematically
  • Rotate the image mentally — hidden blocks matter
Max faces = 6 per block | Count only shared face contact, not edge/corner
12. Aviation Information
20 Q / 8 min
Pilot, ABM
  • Study FAA Pilot's Handbook (free at faa.gov) — it's the source
  • Four forces: Lift, Weight, Thrust, Drag — know their relationships
  • Flight controls: ailerons (roll), elevator (pitch), rudder (yaw)
  • Airport markings: numbers = runway heading in tens of degrees
Lift = roll, Pitch = nose up/down, Yaw = nose left/right | Bernoulli: faster air = lower pressure

Aviation Reference

Aviation Information & Instrument Comprehension

This section covers the content tested in Aviation Information (subtest 12) and provides context for Instrument Comprehension (subtest 10).

Four Forces of Flight

  • Lift — acts upward, perpendicular to wing; generated by Bernoulli effect and angle of attack
  • Weight — acts downward; gravity pulling aircraft toward earth
  • Thrust — acts forward; produced by engine/propeller
  • Drag — acts rearward; opposes forward motion
  • Straight, level flight: Lift = Weight, Thrust = Drag
  • Climbing: Thrust > Drag, Lift > Weight

Flight Control Surfaces

  • Ailerons — on trailing edge of wings; control roll (bank)
  • Elevator — on tail horizontal stabilizer; controls pitch (nose up/down)
  • Rudder — on tail vertical stabilizer; controls yaw (nose left/right)
  • Flaps — increase lift and drag for takeoff/landing
  • Spoilers — reduce lift, increase drag
  • Trim tabs — reduce pilot control pressure

Axes of Rotation

  • Longitudinal axis — nose to tail; roll motion; ailerons
  • Lateral axis — wingtip to wingtip; pitch motion; elevator
  • Vertical axis — top to bottom; yaw motion; rudder
  • Coordinated turn: aileron + rudder together
  • Adverse yaw: aileron input alone causes unwanted yaw opposite bank

Airport Runway Markings

  • Runway numbers = magnetic heading ÷ 10 (rounded)
  • Runway 18 = heading 180° (south)
  • Runway 27 = heading 270° (west)
  • Parallel runways: L (left), R (right), C (center)
  • Threshold markings: white stripes at runway end
  • Taxiways: yellow centerline; hold-short markings = double dashed lines

Instrument Comprehension Guide

  • Artificial Horizon: horizon bar shows sky/ground relationship
  • Bar tilted right = aircraft in left bank (right wing high)
  • Bar tilted left = aircraft in right bank (left wing high)
  • Bar above center = nose pitched down
  • Bar below center = nose pitched up
  • Compass: 0°/360°=N, 90°=E, 180°=S, 270°=W

Weather & Atmosphere

  • Standard atmosphere: 15°C / 59°F at sea level, pressure 29.92 inHg
  • Temperature decreases ~2°C per 1,000 ft gain (standard lapse rate)
  • Clouds: Cumulus (vertical), Stratus (horizontal), Cirrus (high-altitude, icy)
  • Cumulonimbus: thunderstorm clouds — avoid flight through
  • VFR: Visual Flight Rules — weather allows visual navigation
  • IFR: Instrument Flight Rules — navigate by instruments only

Aerodynamics Basics

  • Bernoulli's Principle: faster airflow = lower pressure
  • Wing camber (curved shape) creates faster airflow over top = lift
  • Angle of Attack: angle between wing chord and relative wind
  • Stall: exceeds critical angle of attack; lift collapses
  • Recovery from stall: reduce angle of attack (push nose down)
  • Glide ratio: horizontal distance per unit of altitude lost

Compass Headings — Quick Reference

  • N = 0° / 360°
  • NE = 45°
  • E = 90°
  • SE = 135°
  • S = 180°
  • SW = 225°
  • W = 270°
  • NW = 315°
